Sri Lanka Budget 2027

The government plans to collect Rs 6.18 trillion, spend Rs 7.67 trillion, and borrow the difference. Here is what that means, what changes for you, and which claims in the news hold up.

This is a plan, not law yet. President Anura Kumara Dissanayake, who is also Finance Minister, presented it on 12 November 2026. Parliament votes on 20 November, with the final vote on 14 December. Details can still change.

Independent, non-partisan summary. Not affiliated with the Government of Sri Lanka, any political party or any news outlet. Researched and written with AI assistance. Covers the budget as presented on 12 November 2026; last checked 14 November 2026. 1 trillion = 1,000 billion.

The whole budget in three bars

Money coming in (taxes, fees, grants)Rs 6,180 billion
Money going outRs 7,673 billion + 260 cyclone
The gap, covered by borrowingRs 1,493 billion + 260 cyclone

The gap is called the budget deficit. It equals 3.8% of everything the country produces in a year (GDP). The striped part is a separate Rs 260 billion request to finish rebuilding after Cyclone Ditwah. With it, spending is Rs 7,933 billion and the gap is Rs 1,753 billion, or 4.5% of GDP.

Bars are drawn to the same scale. Source: Budget Speech 2027, Annexure III.

Think of it as a Rs 100 note

Out of every Rs 100 the government spends

  • Rs 34Interest on past loans. The single biggest cost.
  • Rs 23Building things: roads, railways, hospitals, schools, water.
  • Rs 19Subsidies and payments to people, including pensions and Aswesuma.
  • Rs 18Salaries of public servants.
  • Rs 5Running costs: supplies, fuel, services.

Rounded, so the parts add to Rs 99.

Out of every Rs 100 the government collects

  • Rs 58Taxes on what you buy, such as VAT and excise. Everyone pays these, rich or poor.
  • Rs 22Income tax from people and companies.
  • Rs 12Taxes on imports and exports.
  • Rs 8Fees, profits from state bodies, and foreign grants.

Interest alone takes about Rs 43 of every Rs 100 the government collects. That is far lower than in 2022, when it took Rs 79, but it still leaves less for everything else.

Higher than the Rs 34 above because it is out of what the government collects, which is less than it spends. Our calculation from Annexure III.

What changes for you

From 13 November 2026

Lower import tax on some foods

Sprats: Rs 100 down to Rs 10 a kilo. Maldive fish: Rs 302 down to Rs 10. Dried chillies: down by Rs 50. Shop prices may not fall by the same amount.
From 13 November 2026

Higher import tax on sugar

Up by Rs 10 a kilo, to protect local sugar producers.
From April 2027

Less income tax on salaries

Monthly pay up to Rs 162,500 is tax free, up from Rs 150,000. Tax bands get wider and a new 12% band is added. See the table below.
From April 2027

No VAT on books

Exercise books, printed books, dictionaries and children's colouring books become VAT free.
Mostly from July 2027

Higher allowances for some jobs

Doctors, nurses and other health staff, police, armed forces, prison officers, university lecturers and judges. Grama Niladharis and other field officers from April. Health, lecturer and judicial increases are paid half in 2027 and half in 2028.
From 1 July 2027

Some pensioners

Rs 2,500 a month extra, only for people who retired between 2020 and 2024. It stops once their pensions are recalculated, planned to start in 2028.
During 2027

Public servants

The last of three steps of the pay rise announced in 2025 is paid. Rs 110 billion is set aside. This is not a new raise.
During 2027

Welfare

Rs 230 billion for Aswesuma and related payments. Allowances for kidney patients and people with disabilities rise from Rs 10,000 to Rs 12,500 a month.
During 2027

Loans and solar

Rs 174 billion in low-interest loans through banks, for small businesses, rural entrepreneurs, women-headed households, fisheries businesses, home-stay operators and paddy mill owners. Households that newly install rooftop solar with battery storage get a subsidy of Rs 25,000 per kW of battery, for up to 10 kW (a maximum of Rs 250,000).
During 2027

Sri Lankans abroad

Free dual citizenship for the spouse, children and third-generation children of applicants. Housing loans up to Rs 5 million for migrant workers with part of the interest paid by the state.

A fall does not always mean a cut to regular services. The 2026 figures include extra money Parliament approved during that year through supplementary estimates (five by the end of September 2026). Defence running costs, for example, rise slightly (Rs 384 billion to Rs 388 billion); the drop is in its capital line, which was Rs 216 billion in 2026, against Rs 56 billion spent in 2025 and Rs 70 billion planned for 2027. We did not trace what each one-off 2026 amount paid for. Source: Treasury Draft Budget Estimates 2027, Volume I, table 4.4.

  • Debt costs are not in this list.Rs 2,641 billion of interest, Rs 2,204 billion of loan repayments and Rs 82 billion of widows' and orphans' pensions are paid under other laws, so Parliament does not vote on them here. Leaving out the interest is the main reason this total is smaller than the Rs 7.67 trillion budget.
  • The Finance Ministry is not spending this on itself.Rs 362 billion of its total sits with the Department of National Budget, which passes money on to other parts of government during the year.
  • Public Administration is mostly pensions.Rs 550 billion of its Rs 651 billion pays public service pensions.
  • Defence and police are separate lines.Defence includes the Army (Rs 240 billion), Navy (Rs 97 billion) and Air Force (Rs 79 billion). The Police (Rs 167 billion) sit under Public Security.
  • Health and education are bigger than their ministry lines.Provincial councils also run many hospitals and schools, paid from the Provincial Councils money.
  • These numbers can still move.The bill was published in September 2026, before the Budget Speech. Parliament can change individual lines until the final vote on 14 December.

All figures read directly from the First and Second Schedules of the Appropriation Bill. The 27 groups add up exactly to the bill's total of Rs 4,992,773,388,000.

Read the fine print

These points are easy to miss in headlines. All come from the official documents except one, which is marked.

  • Much of the tax-free increase just keeps up with prices.The limit goes up 8.3%, the same as the latest yearly inflation figure (8.3% in October 2026).
  • One tax band gets smaller.The lowest 6% band shrinks from Rs 1,000,000 to Rs 850,000 a year. Everyone still pays less overall because the next bands are wider and start at 12% instead of 18%.
  • The gap is widening again.The deficit was 2.3% of GDP in 2025 and 3.7% in 2026. The plan for 2027 is 3.8%, or 4.5% with cyclone costs. The government's own ceiling is 5%.
  • Income falls short of the government's own goal.The speech aims for revenue of at least 16% of GDP. The 2027 plan shows 15.8%.
  • The cyclone money is not new money.The Rs 260 billion is the unspent part of Rs 500 billion Parliament already approved for Ditwah rebuilding. It will come from cash the Treasury holds.
  • Record building plans depend on delivery.Rs 1,750 billion is planned for public investment, up from Rs 1,480 billion in 2026. Plans are not always carried out in full: EconomyNext reports that in past years only 54% to 76% of capital budgets was actually spent. We could not find an official figure to check that range.
  • The numbers assume steady growth and calmer prices.The Treasury's estimates assume the economy grows 4% to 5% in 2027 and that inflation returns to the 5% target by mid-2027. The 7% growth in the speech is a longer-term goal. If growth is weaker, tax income is likely to fall short.
  • Some things become taxable.Capital gains tax would cover crypto and other digital assets, telecom and broadcast licences, and gems, jewellery and precious metals above a limit yet to be set. Coir pith and artemia eggs (used in fish farming) lose their VAT exemption.
  • Most borrowing repays old loans.The borrowing limit is Rs 3,800 billion. Of that, Rs 2,207 billion goes to repaying loans that fall due. Only the deficit is new debt.
  • The IMF programme ends in March 2027.A committee will advise on whether to enter a new one. Nothing is decided.
  • Prices and weather are the big risks.Inflation rose from 2.1% in December 2025 to 8.3% in October 2026, driven by fuel costs from the Middle East war. An El Niño is expected to peak in February 2027, which can hurt harvests and hydropower.
  • The tax cuts are small next to the budget.They cost Rs 28.4 billion in a full year, about half of one percent of what the government collects.
  • The tourism levy ends, but its replacement is undecided.The Treasury will fund tourism promotion until a new method is designed.

We checked the headlines

News reports gave different numbers for the same budget. We compared each claim with the official documents. Claims are summarised in our own words, not quoted; the headline each one came from is linked below it.

Misleading
"Total government spending is Rs 9.92 trillion."The number is real, but it is everything the government must pay out in 2027, including Rs 2,207 billion to repay loans that fall due. Repaying a loan is not new spending, and these repayments are mostly covered by new borrowing. The budget's own spending figure is Rs 7,673 billion. The Rs 9,920 billion adds up as Rs 5,070 billion for services and projects, Rs 2,643 billion of interest and Rs 2,207 billion of repayments (Annexure IV).
